Report-ready English Summary

The wording avoids causal claims and emphasizes simulated data limitations.

In the simulated dataset, 10 Chinese-background Japanese learners completed a single-group pre-test, AI-assisted learning session, immediate post-test, and delayed post-test workflow. The median total memory score increased from 49.3 before learning to 57.3 immediately after the session, with a mean total gain of 9.78. The delayed median score was 53.5, and the average total retention rate was 93.02%. Changes by memory dimension were as follows: Recognition: median 56.5 to 71.5, W=0, p=0.005, r=0.89 (large). Recall: median 45 to 50.5, W=1, p=0.007, r=0.85 (large). Contextual: median 49 to 57, W=0, p=0.008, r=0.89 (large). Conversation: median 45.5 to 49.5, W=2.5, p=0.011, r=0.81 (large). Grammar: median 50.5 to 56.5, W=1, p=0.007, r=0.85 (large). The mean total gain was 9.78 with an interquartile range of 5.2. Questionnaire responses were generally positive: Perceived Usefulness: median 4, 80% selected 4 or 5; Feedback Clarity: median 4, 80% selected 4 or 5; Learning Engagement: median 4, 80% selected 4 or 5; Confidence Improvement: median 4, 70% selected 4 or 5; Cognitive Load: median 3, 30% selected 4 or 5; Intention to Reuse: median 4, 80% selected 4 or 5. These results demonstrate the analytical workflow for a small-sample educational experiment. Because the dataset is simulated, includes only ten participants, lacks a control group, and represents a short two-hour intervention, the findings should not be interpreted as proving AI effectiveness or establishing long-term educational impact.
